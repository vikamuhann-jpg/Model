Yes. I would give that person a **separate research track** that is intentionally open-ended. Their job should **not** be to copy our baseline or immediately implement another algorithm. Their job is to investigate whether a substantially different modelling approach can outperform, complement, or simplify the existing baseline.

You can give them this brief:

---

# Research Track B — Alternative AML Graph/ML Model

## 1. Objective

Research and identify **an alternative graph/ML approach for AML fund-flow detection** that can be fairly compared with our primary baseline.

The goal is **not necessarily to beat the baseline on accuracy**.

The alternative model may be better in:

* detection quality
* minority-class performance
* false-positive reduction
* unseen-pattern detection
* latency
* throughput
* memory usage
* scalability
* explainability
* robustness
* ability to capture temporal/complex fund-flow behaviour

The final recommendation must be based on experiments, not assumptions.

---

Our primary research pipeline will use:

```text
IBM AMLSim

Graph construction
      ↓
Existing graph-feature approach / GFP baseline
      ↓
XGBoost
      ↓
AML detection
```

Your task is to investigate:

> **What alternative representation, graph algorithm, feature-extraction method, or ML architecture could provide additional value?**

Do not assume that the new method must replace GFP.

It may instead be:

```text
GFP
 +
Alternative method
```

```text

```


# 3. Research Question

Start broad.

Investigate questions such as:

> Can another graph representation capture AML fund-flow behaviour that conventional graph features miss?

> Can temporal information be represented more effectively?

> Can we reduce computational cost while preserving detection quality?

> Can a model detect previously unseen AML patterns better?

> Can a graph-learning method provide richer information than handcrafted graph features?

> Can we achieve similar detection quality with a simpler/faster model?

The researcher should eventually reduce these to **one clear hypothesis**.

# 5. Important Factors for OUR Case

These factors are mandatory because this isn't a generic graph benchmark.

## Factor 1 — AML typologies

The method should be evaluated against the PS9 scenarios:

```text
Layering
Round-tripping
Fan-in
Fan-out
Dormant-account activation
Structuring-like fragmented behaviour
Profile mismatch
Composite patterns
```

---

## Factor 2 — Temporal behaviour

Time matters.

The researcher must investigate:

* time between transfers
* transaction bursts
* acceleration
* ordering
* duration of fund movement

A model that ignores time should clearly justify why.

---

## Factor 3 — Highly imbalanced data

Fraud/AML cases are usually rare compared with legitimate activity.

Do not optimize for:

> Accuracy

alone.

Focus on:

* PR-AUC
* Recall
* Precision
* F1
* Precision@K
* false positives

---

## Factor 4 — Hard legitimate cases

This is mandatory.

Test against legitimate flows that may look suspicious:

```text
Payroll
Treasury movement
Merchant settlements
Large business transfers
Normal fan-out
Normal high-volume accounts
```

The model should not simply detect:

> “complex graph = fraud.”

---

## Factor 5 — Unseen patterns

The researcher should design at least one test where the model does not see the exact pattern during training.

Example:

```text
Training:
Layering
Cycle

Testing:
Dormant activation + layering + fan-out
```

Question:

> Can the model generalize beyond the patterns it was explicitly trained on?

---

# 6. Fair Comparison Rules

This is extremely important.

The alternative model must use:

### Same dataset

**IBM AMLSim** initially.

### Same train/validation/test split

Do not create a more favourable split.

### Same preprocessing principles

Unless preprocessing itself is the research contribution.

### Same ground truth

Do not change labels.

### Same test cases

Every model gets the same challenge.

### Same hardware

Measure performance under the same environment.

### Same evaluation metrics

Otherwise the comparison is not valid.

# 8. Do Not Assume “More Complex = Better”

This is a critical instruction.

Suppose:

```text
GFP + XGBoost
PR-AUC = 0.86
Latency = 100 ms
```

and a GNN gives:

```text
PR-AUC = 0.88
Latency = 1.8 sec
GPU = required
```

The GNN is **not automatically the better solution**.

For a real-time banking system, the trade-off matters.

The researcher should report:

```text
Accuracy
vs
Latency
vs
Compute
vs
Memory
vs
Complexity
```

# 13. The Researcher's Most Important Question

Ask them to investigate:

> **What information does our existing baseline fail to capture?**

Not:

> “What fancy algorithm can we add?”

For example, they might discover:

```text
GFP captures topology well
but weakly represents temporal acceleration
```

Then their solution should target **temporal representation**.

Or:

```text
GFP is efficient
but shallow graph context limits complex-pattern detection
```

Then perhaps investigate **adaptive multi-hop modelling**.

Or:

```text
GNN improves recall
but latency is too high
```

Then investigate a **lightweight graph representation**.

That is how we should find the research direction.

### Outcome B — Efficiency improvement

```text
+

```

### Outcome C — Generalization improvement

```text

```

### Outcome D — False-positive improvement



Even:

> “This method does not improve the baseline under controlled experiments.”

is scientifically useful.

We simply don't use it in the final system.

# 17. Give them this exact research challenge

## **Research Challenge B**

> **Investigate whether an alternative graph representation, graph-learning algorithm, temporal modelling technique, or adaptive graph-feature extraction strategy can improve upon the existing AML baseline for complex fund-flow detection.**
>
> The research must determine **where the baseline loses information or efficiency**, propose a technically motivated alternative, and evaluate it on the **same AMLSim dataset, same temporal data split, same ground truth and same evaluation environment**.
>
> The goal is not necessarily to maximize accuracy. The objective is to identify the best **accuracy–false-positive–generalization–latency–compute trade-off** for real-time banking AML.

---

## And I would give them one final instruction

> **“Don't come back with a model name. Come back with a problem that the current model cannot solve well, evidence showing why, a candidate method that should solve it, and an experiment that can prove or disprove the claim.”**

That will keep their research complementary to your **M0 baseline**, rather than creating a second disconnected project.
